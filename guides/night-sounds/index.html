What sounds do you make in your sleep?

Most people have no idea what they sound like at night. Here are the three sounds worth knowing about—and how to catch them.

Snoring

Snoring is the vibration of soft tissue in your airway. It gets louder when you sleep on your back, after alcohol, or when your nose is blocked. Occasional snoring is normal; what matters is the pattern across nights, which is why tracker apps show trends instead of a single night.

Sleep talking (somniloquy)

Sleep talking is more common than people think—it can happen in any sleep stage, from mumbled words to full sentences. It is harmless, often funny, and usually forgotten by morning. Recording it is the only way to actually hear it.

Night coughs

Coughs at night can come from a dry throat, dust, or a lingering cold. Compared with snores they are short and sharp, which makes them easy to separate with sound analysis.

How an iPhone app detects them privately

  • Sound is analysed on device—audio is not uploaded for review.
  • Silence is never intentionally stored. Only short clips of detected events are kept.
  • Events are labelled as likely snoring, possible sleep talking, or cough-like sounds, then shown on a night timeline.

A quiet bedroom microphone plus on-device classification is enough: you review a few short clips in the morning instead of hours of raw audio.

Quick answers

How do I know if I snore? Run a tracker beside your bed and replay the clips in the morning—memory is not reliable here.

Is snoring dangerous? Simple snoring is common and usually harmless. If you notice breathing pauses or heavy daytime sleepiness, talk to a doctor. A tracker is wellness information, not a diagnosis.
